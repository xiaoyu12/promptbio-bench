Optimal k = 3. Consensus NMF (init='nndsvda', 50 runs per k, KMeans n_init=1 on W, seeds from RandomState(42)) gives cophenetic correlation of 1.0 for both k=2 and k=3, then 0.999 for k=4, falling to 0.93 by k=10. k=3 splits the samples into three perfectly stable 30/30/30 clusters. k=2 just merges two of them (60/30), so the tie was broken by taking the largest k before cophenetic correlation drops (Brunet et al.).
